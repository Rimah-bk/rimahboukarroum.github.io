AI Document Assistant

A local-first PDF assistant that lets you upload a technical document and ask questions about it in plain language — without the file, or the questions, ever leaving your machine.

Why local-first

Cloud-based AI assistants are convenient, but they mean uploading whatever document you're studying to a third party — not ideal for lecture notes, lab reports, or anything else you'd rather keep off someone else's server. This project runs the language model itself on the local machine through Ollama, so the whole question-answering loop — reading the PDF, reasoning about it, and generating a response — happens without a network round trip.

How it works

  • Upload — a PDF lesson is dropped into the app and its text is extracted locally.
  • Ask — a question is typed in about the document's content, no special syntax required.
  • Ground the answer — the relevant parts of the document are pulled in as context so the model answers from the actual text instead of guessing.
  • Respond, locally — the question and context are handed to a model served by Ollama on the same machine, and the answer streams back in the interface.

The interface itself is built with Streamlit, which made it fast to iterate on the upload → ask → answer flow without hand-building a front end.

What it's for

It's aimed squarely at studying from long, dense technical PDFs — the kind where the answer to "wait, what did this mean again?" is buried forty pages back. Instead of re-reading, you ask, and the assistant finds it for you.
