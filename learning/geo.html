AI Search Content

GEO

Generative Engine Optimization — shaping content so that AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Copilot) retrieve it, ground their answers in it, and cite it as a source.

What it is

GEO is SEO's counterpart for engines that answer a question directly instead of returning a list of links. There is no page of ten blue results to climb — there's one synthesized answer with a handful of citations, and the objective is to be one of those cited sources. It overlaps with SEO (you still need to be crawlable and relevant) but adds concerns specific to how language models retrieve and quote text.

Why it matters

A growing share of queries are now resolved inside an AI answer, and many of those never generate a click to any website. When the model does attribute its answer, the cited sources get the visibility and the trust. Being quotable by these engines is becoming the equivalent of ranking first — and because answers are assembled from short passages, a single well-written section can earn a citation even if the whole page wouldn't rank highly in classic search.

Key concepts

  • Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) — the engine searches an index, pulls back relevant passages, and the LLM writes an answer grounded in them. Optimization targets the retrieval step and the quotability of each passage.
  • Chunk-level relevance — answers are built from passages, not whole pages. Self-contained sections with a clear claim up front travel better than long, meandering prose.
  • Answer-ready writing — definitions first, concrete facts and figures with their source, question-style headings, comparison tables, and short declarative sentences a model can lift verbatim.
  • Citation & attribution — which sources the engine names or links. Corroboration across multiple reputable sites raises the odds of being picked.
  • Entity clarity — consistent names, descriptions, and facts about a person, product, or project across the web, so the model resolves the entity correctly.
  • Training data vs. live retrieval — being in the model's training set influences what it knows; being in the live retrieval index influences what it can cite right now. They are different games.

How it actually works

A typical answer engine takes the user's question, reformulates it into one or more search queries, and runs them against a web index (its own, or a partner such as Bing). It retrieves the top passages, ranks them for relevance and trust, and feeds a selection into the LLM as context. The model then composes an answer, ideally with inline citations back to the passages it used; some engines do a second pass to filter or re-rank sources for reliability. GEO works on three levers: make passages easy to retrieve (phrase things the way people ask questions), easy to quote (concise, factual, self-contained sentences), and safe to trust (named author, visible date, claims that match other reputable sources).
