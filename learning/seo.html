SEO

Search Engine Optimization — the practice of improving a site's visibility in the organic (unpaid) results of search engines, mainly Google, so relevant pages rank higher for the queries people actually type.

What it is

SEO is the work of making a website easy for a search engine to find, understand, and trust. It covers the content itself, the HTML and structure around it, the technical health of the site, and the reputation signals pointing at it from the rest of the web. The goal is durable, no-cost-per-click traffic from people already searching for what the page offers.

Why it matters

Organic search is a compounding channel: a page that ranks keeps pulling in visitors long after it's published, without paying per visit. For a personal or portfolio site it's how people find you when they search your name, your projects, or a problem you've written about. It also forces good habits — fast pages, clear structure, honest content — that help every visitor, not just search bots.

Key concepts

  • Crawling & indexing — bots (Googlebot) follow links to discover pages, render them, and store them in an index. If a page can't be crawled or rendered, it can't rank.
  • Ranking signals — relevance to the query, content quality and depth, links and mentions from other sites (authority), and page experience (Core Web Vitals, mobile-friendliness, HTTPS).
  • On-page SEO — title tags, meta descriptions, a sensible heading hierarchy, semantic HTML, descriptive internal links, image alt text, and structured data (schema.org).
  • Technical SEO — sitemap.xml, robots.txt, canonical URLs, clean URL structure, HTTPS, and fast server responses.
  • Off-page SEO — backlinks and brand mentions that signal other people find the page worth pointing to.
  • Search intent — whether a query is informational, navigational, or transactional, and matching the page to that intent.

How it actually works

The pipeline has two halves. Offline: a crawler fetches pages by following links, a renderer runs the page like a browser, and the text is broken into terms and stored in an inverted index (term → list of pages containing it), along with signals like link counts and freshness. At query time: the engine expands and interprets the query (synonyms, intent, entities — using ML systems such as RankBrain and BERT), retrieves candidate pages from the index, scores them with a stack of ranking systems, and returns a ranked list — often wrapped in features like featured snippets, "People also ask", sitelinks, and image or video packs. SEO is the practice of improving the inputs to that scoring: be crawlable, be relevant, be trustworthy, be fast.
